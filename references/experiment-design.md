# Seven-Day Experiment Design

Design one experiment around the selected bottleneck. Avoid bundles of unrelated changes because they prevent useful interpretation.

## Required Contract

Every experiment must specify:

1. **Decision:** what the shop will decide after the experiment.
2. **Hypothesis:** the expected directional change and why it is plausible.
3. **Primary intervention:** one change under the shop's control.
4. **Primary metric:** one measurable outcome with an explicit formula.
5. **Baseline:** the comparable pre-experiment value and period.
6. **Target:** a directional or numeric threshold. Mark uncited targets as planning choices, not benchmarks.
7. **Guardrails:** at least one metric that must not materially worsen.
8. **Daily actions:** owner, due date, and completion evidence.
9. **Stop condition:** safety, cost, margin, service, or data-quality condition that ends the test.
10. **Review date:** when the next period will be analyzed.

## Good Experiment Shapes

- Change a preparation or handoff step during one time window.
- Bundle one slow-moving item with one popular item while preserving a margin floor supplied by the user.
- Rewrite one expectation-setting message to reduce a recurring complaint.
- Add one structured feedback question to receipts or pickup messages.

## Avoid

- Changing price, staffing, menu, promotion, and opening hours at once.
- Treating a seven-day change as proof of long-term causality.
- Optimizing revenue while ignoring refunds, margin, wait time, or complaint rate.
- Publishing customer text without anonymization or permission.
- Recommending unsafe food-handling, employment, tax, or legal actions.

## Comparison Language

Use `increased`, `decreased`, or `was unchanged within rounding`. Follow with: `This before/after association does not by itself establish causality.` List at least one competing explanation such as weekday mix, weather, stock availability, seasonality, or incomplete records.
